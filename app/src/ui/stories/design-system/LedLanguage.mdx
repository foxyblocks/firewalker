# LED language

The remote controls a strip of LEDs, so it draws its controls as LEDs. A row of square pixels, lit or dark, is the app's signature, and it should feel like the strip in your hand.

## Three ways a row of pixels speaks

**Fill** lights every pixel up to a value. Brightness uses it.

<Specimen><Brightness /></Specimen>

**Point** lights one pixel. A strip marker, such as the toe, uses it, in the marker's own strip color.

<Specimen><Toe /></Specimen>

**Pool** lights from the center toward a side. Tilt uses it, which is roughly what rainbow-pool does on the boot.

<Specimen>
  <TiltStrip tilt={0.45} />
  <div className="strip-ends"><span>Heel down</span><span>Toe down</span></div>
</Specimen>

## Rules

- A lit pixel glows in its own color. An unlit pixel is `--line` and never glows.
- Ends are always labeled, such as Heel down and Toe down, because a row alone doesn't say which way is which.
- A warning outlines pixels in `--wait` rather than coloring them, so the level still reads. The soft cap uses this.
- Ember pixels are the app's own controls. Blue, yellow, and purple pixels are the strip's layout colors, and nothing else.
